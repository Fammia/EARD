Abstract

Implicit feedback is central to modern recommender systems but is inherently noisy, often impairing model training and degrading user experience. Existing denoising strategies typically overlook the entity-specific nature of noise while introducing high computational costs and complex hyperparameter tuning.

We propose EARD (Entity-Aware Reliability-Driven Denoising), a lightweight framework that shifts the focus from interaction-level signals to entity-level reliability. EARD quantifies user and item reliability via their average training losses and integrates these with interaction-level confidence. The framework is model-agnostic, computationally efficient, and requires only two intuitive hyperparameters.

Three Challenges We Address

01

No Entity-Aware Modeling

Existing methods analyze noise only at interaction level, ignoring that noise originates from specific users ("wanderers") or items ("clickbait").

02

High Computational Cost

Methods like DeCA and BOD require up to 3× more GPU memory via meta-learning or bi-level optimization — impractical at industrial scale.

03

Excessive Hyperparameters

DeCA needs 3, UDT needs 4, SGDL needs 5 hyperparameters. EARD uses only α and β with a smooth, unimodal performance landscape.

Reliability Score

✖️
Weight Fusion
w = w_base · w_user · w_item

Why ECDF for Base Weights?

Loss distributions are often non-standard and long-tailed. ECDF is distribution-agnostic, robust to outliers, and produces smooth weights in [0,1] without hard thresholds.

Why Average Loss as Reliability Proxy?

Noisy interactions tend to produce higher training losses. Entities with high average loss across interactions are less reliable — a signal that is both simple and empirically validated.
